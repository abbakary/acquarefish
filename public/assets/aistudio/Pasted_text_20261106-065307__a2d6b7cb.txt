Digital Fish Buying, Trading & Supply Chain Management System for Tanzania
This is a very good software opportunity in Tanzania. You can help fish-buying companies move from paper-based operations to a centralized digital management system that tracks fish purchased from fishermen, stock received, quality, payments, transportation, sales to industries, and business profitability.

I would recommend developing a Fish Procurement, Inventory & Supply Chain Management System.

The system should be designed for companies that do not necessarily catch fish themselves. Instead, they buy fish from fishermen or fishing groups, collect it at landing sites, store or process it, and then sell it to fish-processing industries, factories, exporters, or other buyers.

1. Understand the actual business workflow

A typical business operates like this:

Fishermen bring fish from fishing activities.

The company buys fish from individual fishermen or fishing groups.

Staff weigh the fish and record the quantity.

Staff classify fish by species, size, and quality.

The company pays the fishermen.

Fish is transported to a storage facility or processing location.

Fish is sold and delivered to industries.

The company receives payments and calculates profit.

The problem with paper management: It becomes difficult to know exactly how much fish was purchased, who supplied it, how much was paid, how much was lost, how much remains in stock, and how much profit was generated.

Your software should solve these problems from the point of purchase to the final sale.

3. The most valuable feature: Complete fish traceability
I would make this the central feature that differentiates your solution.

Every fish purchase should have a unique batch number, and that batch should follow the fish throughout the business.

Who inspected it.

9. Additional features that could make the system competitive
Once the basic system works, introduce:

Automatic profit calculation: Calculate profit per fish species, supplier, batch and customer.

Supplier performance: Identify suppliers who consistently deliver quality fish at competitive prices.

Fish price management: Compare buying and selling prices across different landing sites.

Stock loss detection: Identify unusual differences between received, stored and sold quantities.

Management alerts: Notify the owner when stock is spoiling, customer debts are overdue or supplier payments are pending.

Business forecasting: Estimate purchasing requirements based on historical demand.

Digital supplier portal: Allow suppliers to view their purchases and payment statements.

Customer portal: Allow industries to submit orders and track deliveries.

10. How to approach the company before developing
My strongest advice is to visit the company and observe its actual operations before deciding on the final features.

Ask the owner these questions:

How do you currently record fish purchases?

Do you buy directly from fishermen or through agents?

Do you operate at multiple landing sites?

How do you weigh and classify different fish species?

How do you calculate payments to fishermen?

Where do you store purchased fish?

Do you experience differences between purchased quantities and sold quantities?

Do you sell to one industry or multiple industries?

Do industries pay immediately or on credit?

How do you currently calculate your actual profit?

What reports does the owner need every day?

You can request to see their existing purchase books, weighing records, payment vouchers, stock registers and sales invoices. These documents will help you translate their real working processes into software.

Build this as a complete Fish Trading and Supply Chain ERP, not just an inventory system or a fish marketplace.
